## 1. OVATION Prime

**Coupling function (Verified, code):** dΦ/dt = v^(4/3) · B_T^(2/3) · sin^(8/3)(θc/2), v in km/s, B_T = sqrt(By²+Bz²) nT, θc = atan2(By_GSM, Bz_GSM). Solar-cycle mean ≈ 4421 (km/s)^(4/3) nT^(2/3) (`dFave = 4421` in OvationPyme; auroramaps cites Newell et al. 2010).

**4-hour weighted average (Verified, code — paper text not accessible):** hourly means of the 4 preceding hours; weight of hour k back = 0.65^k → 1, 0.65, 0.4225, 0.2746, normalized (sum 2.347). Applied to Bx, By, Bz, V and to the hourly coupling values themselves (`avgsw['Ec']`). NOAA's IDL (`ap_inter_sol.pro`, reproduced in auroramaps `util.py:82-115`) additionally weights the current partial hour by its elapsed fraction a (IDL used sqrt(a)). Source: `lkilcommons/OvationPyme/ovationpyme/ovation_utilities.py` lines 134–181; `helioforecast/auroramaps/auroramaps/util.py`.

**Flux model per bin (Verified, code):** for each season × type (diff, mono, wave, ions) × bin (0.25° MLAT × 15 min MLT, 50–90°, grid 80×96 per hemisphere): flux = (b1 + b2·dΦ/dt) · p, p = probability = clip(b1p + b2p·dΦ/dt, 0, 1) or tabulated in 12 dΦ/dt bins of width 4421/8 = 552.6 (index capped → probability saturates above ≈6600). Seasons blended linearly by DOY (centers 79/171/263/354). OP-2010 "saturation" is post-hoc clipping (`correct_flux`): electron energy flux > 10 → 0.5, 5–10 → 5 erg cm⁻² s⁻¹; ions > 2 → 2. Fit data header: 1984–2005 DMSP SSJ (Machol poster: 1985–2006).

**OP-2013 (Newell, Liou, Zhang, Sotirelis, Space Weather 12, 368, 2014, doi:10.1002/2014SW001056) — abstract Verified, mechanics Unverified:** uses TIMED/GUVI UV for dΦ/dt > 1.2 MWb/s (≈Kp 5+/6−); validity 0 < dΦ/dt ≤ 3.0 MWb/s (≈Kp 8+); reduced salt-and-pepper noise; smoother postmidnight-gap interpolation; over the common range OP-2010 and OP-2013 give essentially identical hemispheric power. Conversion between MWb/s and the 4421-unit system: Unverified.

**Hemispheric power vs coupling:** no closed form is published; HP = area integral of flux. Computed here with OvationPyme (OP-2010, N hemisphere, equinox, all 4 types), Verified by running:

≈ HP ≈ 7.4 + 0.00435·dΦ/dt GW (diffuse ≈ 55–60% of total). Newell 2009 (doi:10.1029/2009JA014326, abstract Verified): diffuse = 84% of energy flux at low driving (63% e⁻, 21% ions), 71% at high; broadband rises ×8.

**NOAA operational (Verified, spaceweather.gov/products/aurora-30-minute-forecast):** "OVATION 2020" since 2020; lead time = L1 travel time; Kp-driven fallback with zero lead. Probability (Case et al. 2016 Eq. 1, Verified): P(%) = 10 + 8·Σj (electron types only, j in erg cm⁻² s⁻¹), clipped at 100. `json/ovation_aurora_latest.json` = 65,160 points `[lon, lat, P%]` (1°×1°), 5-min cadence, keys Observation Time / Forecast Time.

## 2. Kp / Hp30 from solar wind

- **Newell et al. 2008** (JGR 113, A04218, doi:10.1029/2007JA012825): Kp = 0.05 + 2.244×10⁻⁴·dΦ/dt + 2.844×10⁻⁶·n^(1/2)·v² (n cm⁻³, v km/s). Verified via arXiv 1701.06525 quoting it; abstract Verified (best pair dΦ/dt + n^½v² explains 61.0% of variance across 10 indices; viscous term alone 22.3%). Kp-only fit and the averaging window: **Unverified** (paywalled). Newell 2007 (doi:10.1029/2006JA012015, abstract Verified): dΦ/dt explains 57.2% of variance vs 50.9% E_KL, 48.8% vBs; data at "usually 1 h" cadence.
- **Wintoft et al. 2017** (JSWSC 7, A29, doi:10.1051/swsc/2017027, Verified from GFZ PDF): IRF-Kp-2017 (3-h means + 3-h min/max of Bz, n, V, B, By from 1-min data + DOY/UT, NN ensemble median): RMSE 0.55, r 0.92 (2-yr independent ACE L2 test); h3 variant RMSE 0.63, r 0.89; with real-time ACE 0.59/0.73; lead 20–90 min. Wintoft & Wik 2018: L1 travel + ≤1 h lead feasible.
- **Shprits et al. 2019** (Space Weather 17, 1219, doi:10.1029/2018SW002141, Verified from GFZ PDF): SW-driven NN RMSE ≈0.55 at 0 h, ≈0.7 at 3 h; recurrence beats SW beyond ~1 day. GFZ PAGER Kp: OMNI 1998–2019 training, hourly 72-h ensemble (secondary).
- **Tan et al. 2018** (Space Weather 16, 406, doi:10.1002/2017SW001764, abstract Verified): LSTM with SW+IMF+Kp history; abstract prints RMSE 0.4765, MAE 0.6382 (as printed); F1(Kp≥5)=0.55 (secondary).
- **Wing et al. 2005** (doi:10.1029/2004JA010500, abstract Verified): APL NN models 1-h and 4-h ahead with nowcast Kp, plus SW-only 1-h; r≈0.92 for 1-h (secondary).
- Sexton et al. 2019 (JSWSC 9, A19): 24-h MLP, C≈0.82. Chakraborty & Morley 2020 (JSWSC 10, 36): probabilistic 3-h Kp. Zhelavskaya et al. 2019: horizons to 12 h. Abstracts Verified.
- **Hp30**: Kervalishvili et al. 2025 GRL (doi:10.1029/2025GL114848) observatory-level ML for Kp/Hp60/Hp30 (no skill numbers in abstract); Billcliff et al. 2026 Space Weather (doi:10.1029/2025SW004823): BSS_clim 0.595, ROC-AUC 0.751 at 6-h lead for Hp30max ≥ 5 in 24 h. **No published linear Hp30-vs-dΦ/dt regression found** → fit our own (Section 6 data).

## 4. Substorm statistics

- **Freeman & Morley 2004** (GRL 31, L12807, doi:10.1029/2004GL019989; abstract + Morley et al. 2007 Ann. Geophys. 25, 2427 + Freeman & Morley 2009 GRL text, Verified): energy F loads at solar-wind power P; onset when F reaches a fixed threshold (absolute value arbitrary); at onset energy W_i = A_eff·D·P(t_i) is released, so recurrence = D under constant driving. D = 2.6–2.9 h matches 1982–83 geosynchronous waiting times (best 2.69 h; 2.73 h with amplification-limited B). Power input: P = (vB²/μ0)·sin⁴(θ/2) W m⁻² (ε per unit area), A_eff = 4πL0², L0 = 7 R_E (Perreault–Akasofu) or 10 R_E (Koskinen & Tanskanen); B_al = min(B, 60 nT/7).
- **Borovsky, Nemzek, Belian 1993** (JGR 98, 3807, doi:10.1029/92JA02556, abstract Verified): 1001 intervals; most probable Δt ≈ 2.75 h (periodic); random component mean ≈ 5 h; ~1500 substorms/yr, half periodic, half random.
- **Newell & Gjerloev 2011b** (doi:10.1029/2011JA016936, abstract Verified): 53,000 SML substorms 1980–2009; no preferred recurrence, N(Δt) ∝ Δt^−1.19 for short Δt with a break at ~3 h; successive waiting times weakly correlated (r 0.18/0.06); typical peak SME 400–600 nT, mean 656 nT ≈ 40 GW; quiet floor 61 nT ≈ 5 GW.
- **Newell & Gjerloev 2011a** (doi:10.1029/2011JA016779, abstract Verified): SME vs nightside auroral power r = 0.86; 10,719 SML onsets 1997–2002 (5,084 isolated); SML onset = decrease ≥15 nT/min sustained ≥3 min (i.e. 45 nT in 3 min; Verified via Forsyth 2015 text); the "≥100 nT below onset value for the following ~25–30 min" sustain criterion is Unverified (secondary).
- **Morley & Freeman 2007** (GRL 34, L08104, doi:10.1029/2006GL028891, abstract Verified): prior loading necessary, northward turning not; association reproduced by the MSM. Freeman & Morley 2009 (doi:10.1029/2009GL040621, Verified): superposed-epoch northward turn at onset is reproduced by random times with Bz ≤ −0.7 nT and by MSM onsets. Published "triggered fraction" (Hsu & McPherron) not verified here.
- **Durations (Verified):** Partamies et al. 2013 (Ann. Geophys. 31, 349; 15,568 growth / 54,519 expansion phases, AL 1995–2009): median growth 31, expansion 12, recovery 31, quiet 75 min (means 48/24/48/200; modes 14/14/10). Li et al. 2013 (doi:10.1002/jgra.50399; 379 southward turnings): growth minutes–3 h; geometric means 91/62/32 min for weak/moderate/strong dayside reconnection E; onset requires E_rec ≥ 0.6 mV/m and v ≥ 280 km/s; AP maxima 35/51/74 GW. Forsyth et al. 2015 SOPHIE (doi:10.1002/2015JA021343, arXiv 1606.02651; EPT 50/75/90%): median expansion 19/17/14 min, recovery 30/34/33, possible growth 57/78/141 min; waiting times between isolated onsets (<10 h): median 3.9/3.7/3.7 h, SD 2.5/2.35/2.35 h.

## 5. L1 → Earth propagation

- Ballistic: Δt = (X_sc − X_target)/|Vx|. **NOAA propagated product (Verified, computed from the JSON + ephemerides):** target X_GSE = 32 R_E (Geospace model boundary; implied 32.0–32.1 R_E with the active SWFO-L1 "SOLAR1" ephemeris), delays 30.5–88 min (median 56) over the last 7 days; add ≈(32−10) R_E/v ≈ 5 min to reach the magnetopause. Active RTSW source on 2026-09-17: SOLAR1 (SWFO-L1); ACE and IMAP also present.
- Errors (Verified): Mailyan, Munteanu, Haaland 2008 (Ann. Geophys. 26, 2383; 198 ACE→Cluster events): flat delay within ±10 min "in most cases"; MVAB-0 65% within ±5 min, >30% within ±2 (filtered: 82% within ±5). Cameron & Jackel 2016 (doi:10.1002/2016SW001451): MVAB-0 best, ~1 min better than flat shift on average; all methods >5 min worse for radial IMF. Baumann & McCloskey 2021 (JSWSC 11, 41, doi:10.1051/swsc/2021026; 380 shocks 1998–2018): gradient boosting RMSE 4.5 min, beats flat delay by 50% (flat ≈ 9 min RMSE) and vector delay by 15%. Weimer et al. 2003 (doi:10.1029/2002JA009405): MVA needs 7–30 min windows; corrects errors "on the order of 30 min or more". OMNI uses MVAB-0 + cross-product phase-front normals (HROdocum.html).

Space Sci. 10:1250779, Verified): GRU→dense; input 50 × ~100 s (~80 min) of Wind data; 30-min-ahead output at MMS/bow shock; CRPS 0.214σ vs MVA 0.350σ; Bz CRPS 1.07 vs 1.56 nT; Vx 9.23 vs 16.1 km/s; n 0.85 vs 2.16 cm⁻³. Code+weights public: `github.com/connor-obrien888/prime` (GPL-3.0; `prime_lib/primesw/PRIME.ckpt` 3.0 MB, PRIME-SH/PS variants), PyPI `primesw` 2.0.3 (Python 3.8–3.12).
