## 1. Topic 1 — Newell & Gjerloev (2011) SML onset criterion

### Newell, P. T., & Gjerloev, J. W. (2011a). Evaluation of SuperMAG auroral electrojet indices as indicators of substorms and auroral power. *JGR* 116, A12211. doi:10.1029/2011JA016779
- Fetched: `https://web.archive.org/web/20250521050742/https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2011JA016779` (full text) and `https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1029/2011JA016779` (abstract).
- **Criterion, verbatim (section 3.2, para [20]):** "The SML (and AL) data were considered at a 1 min cadence in a sliding 30 min buffer. An onset was identified at t0 when four conditions are satisfied: [equations rendered as images] Thus the drop must be sharp (45 nT in 3 min), and sustained (must average 100 nT below the initial value for the remainder of the half hour). The SML onset is then placed at t0, the last minute before a 15 nT drop. Once an onset is identified, the algorithm advances ahead 20 min, which is thus the minimum permitted time between two consecutive onsets. (In fact, only a relative handful of onsets do occur that close together.)"
- **The four equations, verbatim as reproduced by Maimaiti et al. (2019, accepted manuscript, eqs 1–4)** — fetched `https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10189693`:
  - SML(t0+1) − SML(t0) < −15 nT (1)
  - SML(t0+2) − SML(t0) < −30 nT (2)
  - SML(t0+3) − SML(t0) < −45 nT (3)
  - Σ_{i=4}^{30} SML(t0+i)/26 − SML(t0) < −100 nT (4)
  - followed by: "Thus the drop must be sharp (equations (1)–(3), that is, 45 nT in 3 min), and sustained (equation (4), i.e., must be 100 nT below the initial value for the remainder of the half hour on average). The SML onset is then placed at t0, the last minute before a 15-nT drop. Once an onset is identified, the algorithm advances ahead 20 min, which is thus the minimum permitted time between two consecutive onsets."
  - Note (inferred): as printed the sum runs i = 4…30 (27 samples) but divides by 26; the prose ("remainder of the half hour") implies the mean over minutes 4–29 or 4–30. The difference is immaterial; use the mean of 26 samples (t0+4 … t0+29) or 27 samples — flag as an implementation choice.
- **Separation rule: verified = 20 min lockout** (not 30). The "isolated" bookkeeping is separate: "recurrent substorms (those following less than 2 h after a previous onset)" (abstract, verified). Other lists use different isolation gaps: Frey & Mende 2006 accepted a new event only "if at least 30 min had passed after the previous onset" (Johnson & Wing PPPL report, verified-secondary); Liou et al. 2013 define isolated as "separation of two consecutive onsets no less than 3 h" (Crossref abstract, verified).
- **Onsets per year (verified):** "For 1997, the application of this algorithm to AL(12) data locates 1006 substorms onsets. For the same year and algorithm, 1397 onsets are found in the SML data (39% more)." "There are 10,719 onsets in the SML data between 1 January 1997 and 31 December 2002, of which 5084 are isolated." (= 1,786/yr; 47% isolated; 53% recurrent). Also "SML can be used to identify a significantly larger number of onsets (about 30–40% more per year)". Forsyth et al. 2015 (arXiv, verified) count "30,484 substorms identified by NG11" over 1996–2014 (≈1,600/yr) and note the "NG11 onset threshold (−15 nT/min over at least 3 min) corresponds to the 82nd percentile of the unfiltered [dSML/dt] data set".
- **Timing (verified):** "The same algorithm applied to SML is much more likely (about 50% more likely) to identify an onset seen by UVI than is AL(12). … the median delay after imaging onset until the AL indicator is less than half using SML (about 4 min versus 8 min)."
- **Typical SML minimum:** not stated in this paper's reachable text (**UNVERIFIED here**). Use instead:
  - Newell & Gjerloev (2011b), *JGR* 116, A12232, doi:10.1029/2011JA016936 (OpenAlex abstract, verified): "substorms do have a preferred size, with the typical peak SME magnitude reaching 400–600 nT, but with a mean of 656 nT, corresponding to a bit less than 40 GW AP"; database "of more than 53,000 substorms … covering 1980–2009".
  - Liou et al. (2018), *JGR* doi:10.1002/2017JA024451 (OpenAlex abstract, verified): 53,468 onsets 1983–2009; "substorm intensity PDFs, as represented by the peak value of |SML| … can be fitted by two lognormal distribution functions … largest for CME (292 and 674 nT) and smallest for SSW (265 and 434 nT)".
  - Maimaiti et al. 2019 (verified): forecast recall "is about 75% when all substorms are considered and it reaches ∼95% for substorms with ΔSML ≥ 700".

## 2. Topic 2 — Newell & Liou (2011); Newell et al. (2016); OVATION

### Newell, P. T., & Liou, K. (2011). Solar wind driving and substorm triggering. *JGR* 116, A03229. doi:10.1029/2010JA016139
- Fetched: `https://web.archive.org/web/20250619151736/https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2010JA016139` (full text); `https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1029/2010JA016139`.
- Abstract (verified): "(1) 4861 identifications of substorm onsets from satellite global imagers (Polar UVI and IMAGE FUV); (2) a similar number of otherwise random times chosen with a similar solar wind distribution (slightly elevated driving); (3) completely random times. … Superposed epoch analysis verifies that the mean Bz has a northward turning (or at least averages less southward) starting 20 min before onset. … For Bz, the mean change after onset is +0.14 nT (i.e., IMF becomes more northward), but the standard deviation is σ = 2.8 nT. … For EKL, the change is −15 nT km/s ± 830 nT km/s. … applying the Lyons et al. (1997) trigger criteria (excluding only the prior requirement of 22/30 min Bz < 0, which is often not valid for actual substorms) to these three sets of data shows that 'northward turning triggers' occur in 23% of the random data, 24% of the actual substorms, and after 27% of the random elevations. These results strongly support the idea of Morley and Freeman (2007), that substorms require initial elevated solar wind driving, but that there is no evidence for external triggering. Finally dynamic pressure, p, and velocity, v, show no meaningful variation around onset (although p averages 10% above an 11 year mean)."
- Key Points (verified): "Substorms are not externally triggered", "Solar wind pressure is 10% higher during substorms", "Only a minor Bz < 0 growth phase (5 min) is required".
- Body (verified): "It turns out about 25% of substorms occur for northward IMF, and 37% fail to satisfy the 22/30 requirement." Table 1: "For purely random times, 23% of events show a trigger. In the 20 min preceding substorm onset through 5 min after onset, 24% show a trigger." "Table 3 of Hsu and McPherron indicates that the fully automated application of the Lyons et al. [1997] criteria produced triggers for 29% of events. That is much smaller than the 60% they found by hand identification." "Wild et al. [2009] … less than 25% of the approximately 250 substorms identified from IMAGE global auroral images satisfied the Lyons et al. [1997] criteria." Timing convention: OMNI "prelagged to the bow shock. We added an additional 5 min lag to account for the typical time of propagation through the bow shock to the nose of the magnetopause."
- **Probability of onset as a function of coupling in the preceding hour: NOT in this paper** (it is a superposed-epoch/trigger-rate study). **UNVERIFIED** as attributed; the closest verified statements are the 24%-vs-23% trigger rates and "substorms require initial elevated solar wind driving".

### Newell, P. T., Liou, K., Gjerloev, J. W., Sotirelis, T., Wing, S., & Mitchell, E. J. (2016). Substorm probabilities are best predicted from solar wind speed. *JASTP* 146, 28–37. doi:10.1016/j.jastp.2016.04.019

- Abstract (verified): "a continuous list of substorm onsets compiled from SuperMAG covering January 1, 1997 through December 31, 2007 are studied. … In fact v is a much better predictor of onset probability than is the overall merging rate, with substorm odds rising sharply with v. Some loading by merging is necessary, and frontside merging does increase substorm probability, but nearly as strongly as does v taken alone. Likewise, the effects of dynamic pressure, p, are smaller than simply v taken by itself. Changes in the solar wind matter, albeit modestly. For a given level of v (or Bz), a change in v (or Bz) will increase the odds of a substorm for at least 2 h following the change. A decrease in driving elevates substorm probabilities to a greater extent than does an increase, partially supporting external triggering. Yet current v is the best single predictor of subsequently observing a substorm. … the flow of energy through the magnetosphere is determined by frontside merging, but the burstiness of energy dissipation depends primarily on v."

## 3. Topic 3 — Minimal substorm model, waiting times, triggering statistics

### Freeman, M. P., & Morley, S. K. (2004). A minimal substorm model that explains the observed statistical distribution of times between substorms. *GRL* 31, L12807. doi:10.1029/2004GL019989
- Fetched: `https://web.archive.org/web/20240715124929/https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2004GL019989` (full text) and `https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1029/2004GL019989`.
- Rules (verified; equation images replaced by their stated meaning in brackets): "[8] 1. The substorm is driven by power from the solar wind P, which causes energy in the magnetotail E to accumulate at a rate [dE/dt = P]. [9] 2. At any given time, there exists an energy state F that the magnetotail would like to exist in. … For simplicity, let us assume a linear relationship: [F = C − D·P] where constant C is a critical energy threshold and constant D is related to a substorm periodicity … [10] 3. The magnetotail is constrained by its magnetic topology from adopting the energy state F until it becomes sufficiently stressed with an energy C. At this point, the magnetotail moves to the lower energy state [E → F]. This transition is identified with the substorm onset and expansion phase."
- Unloading (verified): "Integrating equation (1) from immediately after the substorm onset at t_i when E = C − DP(t_i) to the next onset at t_i+1 when E = C … This expresses the fact that the energy dissipated by the ith substorm at time t_i is proportional to the solar wind power i[nput at that time]". Hence ∫_{t_i}^{t_{i+1}} P dt = D·P(t_i) (inferred algebra from the verified statements); under constant P the period is exactly D. "L ∼ DP(t_i)/d ∼ 5 P(t_i) for typical d = 30 min and D = 2.7 h".
- Driver (verified): "[13] The solar wind power input per unit area P is estimated from the epsilon parameter [Perreault and Akasofu, 1978]: … θ is the IMF clock angle defined as θ = arctan(By/Bz)". Initialisation: "[14] The time integration is started at E = C − D⟨P⟩, where ⟨P⟩ is the average of P over the whole Wind dataset. It is also re-started after data gaps from this level."
- Observed waiting-time distribution (Borovsky et al. 1993 data, verified): "The distribution has a mode of ≈3 h, a mean μ = 5.1 h and a standard deviation σ = 4.7 h, reflecting the long tail of the distribution with 16 waiting times longer than 24 h."
- Fit (verified): "The value D = 2.69 h corresponds to the minimum χ2 found for a range of D values between 2.0 h and 3.5 h in steps of 0.01. The χ2(D) curve … is low over the range 2.63 ≤ D ≤ 2.86 h, in which 15 out of 24 χ2 values are less than the 5% significance level"; "χ2 = 27.3 is less than the corresponding χ2 = 46.2 at the 5% significance level"; abstract: "For values of D between 2.6 h and 2.9 h … not significantly different"; "Similar results are obtained using solar wind data from the ACE spacecraft between 1998 and 2002."
- Note: the model has *no* dependence on solar wind speed beyond ε, no refractory period, and no external trigger.

### Morley, S. K., & Freeman, M. P. (2007). On the association between northward turnings of the IMF and substorm onsets. *GRL* 34, L08104. doi:10.1029/2006GL028891
- Fetched: `https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/3113/` and Crossref (abstract, verified): "we argue that a minimum interval of prior loading is a necessary condition for substorm onset, a subsequent northward IMF turning is not necessary, and consequently that an internal trigger from a magnetospheric instability is a necessary and sufficient condition for substorm onset. ### Freeman, M. P., & Morley, S. K. (2009). No evidence for externally triggered substorms based on superposed epoch analysis of IMF Bz. *GRL* 36, L21101. doi:10.1029/2009GL040621

## 4. Topic 4 — Li, Wang & Peng (2013) growth-phase durations

### Li, H., Wang, C., & Peng, Z. (2013). Solar wind impacts on growth phase duration and substorm intensity: A statistical approach. *JGR Space Physics* 118, 4270. doi:10.1002/jgra.50399
- Fetched: `https://web.archive.org/web/20250619152145/https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jgra.50399` (full text) and `https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1002/jgra.50399`.
- Abstract (verified): "A statistical survey of 379 interplanetary magnetic field (IMF) southward turning events during the time period from 1995 to 2011 … Substorm growth phase persists from several minutes up to 2–3 h … The lower limits of solar wind reconnection E-field and bulk speed for substorm occurrence are found to be 0.6 mV/m and 280 km/s, respectively. Similarly, the substorm intensity is linearly correlated to the dayside reconnection E-field. However, it seems to be independent of the amount of dayside geomagnetic flux reconnected and solar wind energy entered into the magnetosphere during the growth phase. Furthermore, all the events are divided into three groups for different averages of dayside reconnection E-field during the growth phase … and the geometric means of growth phase duration and auroral power maximum for these three groups are 91 min, 62 min, 32 min, and 35 GW, 51 GW, 74 GW, respectively."
- **Class boundaries: UNVERIFIED** — the three E-field ranges are rendered as equation images on every copy reachable; the text only gives group sizes: "The first group … contains 128 events; the second group … contains 151 events; and the third group … contains 100 events." The E-field is the Kan–Lee field (their Figure 2 panels: "solar wind electric field proposed by Kan and Lee [1979] E_KL"), averaged over the growth phase. Distributions (verified): group 1 "ranges mainly from 50 to 150 min, peaking at 90 min. The geometric mean is 91 min"; group 2 "20 to 140 min, peaking at 80 min … 62 min"; group 3 "10 to 100 min, peaking at about 20 min … only 32 min".
- Overall (verified): "The growth phase duration ranges from 6 to 175 min, with an average of 70 min." "The linear correlation coefficients between growth phase duration and [⟨E_KL⟩] and [⟨v⟩] are −0.57 and −0.47, respectively." Figure 5 text: "for ΔT greater than 120 min, the [⟨E⟩] and [⟨v⟩] are kept nearly unchanged at 1.2 mV/m and 370 km/s as ΔT increases, suggesting that there must be some thresholds for substorm occurrence. Note that the minimum thresholds … are about 0.6 mV/m and 280 km/s, respectively." — i.e. the 0.6 mV/m / 280 km/s "lower limits" are the observed minima of growth-phase-averaged E_KL and v among the 379 events, not a fitted threshold (inferred reading).
- Definitions (verified): growth onset T_NS = IMF southward turning: "the upstream (1 h interval before T_NS) IMF should be mostly (> 85%) northward with the average value of Bz greater than 1.0 [nT]; the downstream (1 h interval after T_NS) IMF should be mostly (> 85%) southward with the average value of Bz less than −1.0 nT; … ratio of standard deviation and average value less than 0.5; … change of solar wind dynamic pressure should be very small (< 30%) …; no substorm occurs during the upstream period (the mean AL index is greater than −100 nT …); associated with a sudden decrease of AL index within the next 3 h after T_NS." Expansion onset T_AL = "the beginning of the sudden sharp decrease of AL index after IMF southward turning … the AL index is required to drop to less than −100 nT within 1.5 h". Validation vs Frey et al. 2004 IMAGE-FUV onsets: "For most of the events (58%), the time difference is less than 5 min. The geometric mean of ΔT_R is −4.8 min and 4.1 minutes". ΔT = T_AL − T_NS.

- **Is a northward turning necessary? No (verified across):** Morley & Freeman 2007 ("not necessary"); Freeman & Morley 2009 (mean-reversion bias reproduces the superposed-epoch turning); Newell & Liou 2011 (Lyons triggers in 24% of substorms vs 23% random; "about 25% of substorms occur for northward IMF"); Johnson & Wing 2014 ("only a few percent additional information"); McPherron & Chu 2023 ("a pulse of either sign precedes 40% of all onsets"; positive-pulse substorms stronger); Hsu & McPherron 2003/2004 (≈60% triggered by hand-identification, ≈40% not; automated Lyons criteria ≈29% per Newell & Liou's reading). - **Does onset probability increase with integrated loading / time since last onset? ## 8. Recommended algorithm (implementable)

Design principle: onsets are not deterministically predictable from L1 data (Newell & Liou 2011; Maimaiti's PCA overlap), but their *rate* is strongly modulated by (i) whether the tail has been loaded at all, (ii) how much has been loaded since the last unloading (Freeman & Morley), (iii) solar wind speed (Newell 2016), and (iv) the recent history of driving changes (Newell 2016; McPherron & Chu). Model the onset process as a non-homogeneous Poisson process with hazard h(t) [h⁻¹] and report P(onset in next T) = 1 − exp(−∫ h dt). This is exactly the Nakano et al. (2023) formulation, so their Brier-score baseline becomes the validation target.

### 8.1 State variables (updated every minute)
1. Driving at the magnetopause: v, n, B_T, θc → dΦ/dt, ε, E_KL, using the propagated L1 series plus a +5–10 min magnetosheath delay (verified conventions). Keep 3-h ring buffers.
2. `E_load` — MSM energy: `E_load += ε·dt` each minute (units arbitrary; only ratios matter). On a detected onset at t_i: `E_load -= D·ε̄(t_i)` where `ε̄(t_i)` is the mean ε over the 20 min before onset (FM04 uses the instantaneous value; a short mean is a robustness choice, inferred) and D = 2.69 h. Floor at 0. Initialise at `E_load = −D·⟨ε⟩_month` (FM04 start rule) — i.e. after a gap, assume one standard loading is needed.
3. `x = E_load / (D·ε̄_60)` — loading fraction relative to the MSM unloading amount, with `ε̄_60` the mean ε over the last 60 min. Under constant driving x reaches 1 exactly D after the last onset (verified consequence of FM04). x is the "time since last onset" variable, expressed in loading rather than clock time.
4. `τ` — minutes since last detected onset (local or, if available, SuperMAG); `phase` ∈ {quiet, growth, expansion, recovery}.
5. Southward-IMF run length `n_S` = number of the last 30 min with Bz < 0 (Morley & Freeman internal trigger at n_S ≥ 22; Newell & Liou: even 5 min suffices statistically).
6. Change memory (Newell 2016): `Δ_dec(t)` = 1 for 120 min after a decrease in driving (e.g. dΦ/dt falling by > 50 % of its 60-min mean, or Bz turning northward by > 2 nT after n_S ≥ 22 — thresholds inferred), `Δ_inc` likewise for increases.
7. SMC flag: local index enhanced (|IL*| ≥ 200 nT scaled analogue of AE ≥ 200) and steady (no drop > 25 nT/min) for ≥ 90 min (O'Brien 2002; Kissinger list minimum duration).

### 8.2 Hazard
`h(t) = λ_clim(v, dΦ/dt) · G(x) · Q(E_KL, v) · R(τ, phase) · T(Δ) · S(SMC)`

- **λ_clim(v, dΦ/dt)** — the empirical per-hour rate from Newell 2016. Because the published curve values are not accessible, *build the table yourself*: download the SuperMAG substorm list (Newell & Gjerloev criteria, 1997–2017) and OMNI 1-min data; bin the 30-min-averaged v (e.g. 300–350 … 700+ km/s) and dΦ/dt (quantile bins); compute P(≥1 onset in the next 60 min | bin) and convert to a rate λ = −ln(1−P). Normalise so that the time-average equals the climatological 0.17 h⁻¹ (Nakano: 15.6 %/h). Until the table exists, use a placeholder `λ_clim = 0.17·(v/450 km/s)^1.5·min(1, dΦ/dt/2500)^0.5` (**inferred, must be replaced**; only the direction of the dependences is verified).
- **G(x)** — loading gate from the MSM: `G = 0.25 + 0.75·σ((x − 0.9)/0.25)` with σ the logistic function. Rationale (verified facts, inferred parameters): under constant driving the MSM fires at x = 1; observed waiting times are broad (SD 4.7 h, mode ≈ 3 h), so the gate must be soft; N&G 2011b's short-Δt power law and 53 % recurrent onsets mean the hazard must never fall to zero once loading exists.
- **Q(E_KL, v)** — Li 2013 floor: if the 60-min mean E_KL < 0.6 mV/m or v < 280 km/s, `Q = 0.2` (few growth phases ever completed below these values); else `Q = 1`. Above ≈1.2 mV/m and 370 km/s no growth phase in Li's sample exceeded 120 min: enforce this by an extra ramp `G → max(G, σ((τ − 120 min)/30 min))` while driving stays above those values and no onset has occurred (inferred from Fig. 5 statement).
- **R(τ, phase)** — refractory/recurrence: `R = 0` for τ < 20 min (matches the N&G detection lockout); `R = 0.6` for 20 min ≤ τ < 60 min (expansion+recovery: intensifications are frequent — SOPHIE 3.7/day at EPT 75 — but the local index cannot resolve a new onset until the previous bay has partly recovered); `R = 1` afterwards. Values inferred.
- **T(Δ)** — trigger term: `T = 1.3` while `Δ_dec` is active after ≥ 22/30 min of southward IMF, `T = 1.15` while `Δ_inc` is active, else 1. Newell 2016 verifies the sign and the ≥ 2-h duration, not the magnitude; Newell & Liou/Johnson & Wing verify that the effect is small (24 % vs 23 %), so keep the multipliers modest and calibrate.
- **S(SMC)** — `S = 0.5` while the SMC flag is set (steady convection resists onset; ≈1/10 of substorm rate but 58 % of SMCs are prolonged substorms that end in a new onset when driving changes — Walach & Milan; Milan 2019). Inferred value.

Then, for horizons T = 30, 60, 120 min: `P_T = 1 − exp(−Σ h(t+k)·Δt)` where the driving inputs for the future minutes are taken from the propagated L1 series as far as it extends (≈30–90 min, verified) and held at the last value beyond that. Display all three, plus the expected time-to-onset from the MSM (`(D·ε̄_60 − E_load)/ε̄_60`) as a secondary "loading clock", and the calibrated reliability band.

### 8.3 Onset detection on the IMAGE chain
1. Build a local lower-envelope index IL* at 1 min: per station, X minus a quiet-day baseline (the AuroraWatch recipe — mean of the five quietest days of the month, Fourier-smoothed — is verified and cheap; a 24-h running median is a simpler fallback); IL* = min over stations. Use only stations within the auroral latitude band relevant to the viewer.
2. Provisional onset ("breakup alarm", latency 3 min): IL*(t0+1), (t0+2), (t0+3) below IL*(t0) by 15, 30, 45 nT (N&G). Confirmed onset (latency 30 min): mean IL* over minutes 4–29 at least 100 nT below IL*(t0). 20-min lockout after each confirmed onset. Report the epoch t0 and note that magnetic onsets lag optical breakup by a median ≈ 4 min.
3. Secondary detectors for slow or weak bays: Tanskanen 80 nT drop within 15 min; Juusola/Partamies dIL*/dt < −4 nT/min with minimum < −50 nT (flag as "weak activation", not a substorm).
4. Phase machine (SOPHIE semantics): expansion from t0 to the IL* minimum (expect median ≈ 17 min); recovery until IL* > −100 nT (Ohtani & Gjerloev end rule) or a new onset; growth otherwise. Intensity class from the IL* minimum (≈ −300 to −700 nT is a typical global peak; local values can be larger).
5. Because the chain sees ≈ 1/3 of global onsets (517 vs ≈1,600 per year), treat "no local detection" as weak evidence: reset `E_load` only on a local detection or on an external SML onset if a SuperMAG feed is added; otherwise let the loading gate saturate (x capped at 2) so the hazard stays at the climatological level rather than growing without bound.

### 8.4 Calibration and validation
1. Hindcast 1997–2017 with OMNI + the public SuperMAG onset list (N&G criteria) and the SOPHIE EPT-75 list (UCL RDR); fit the free parameters (gate centre/width, R, T, S, λ_clim table) by maximum likelihood of the Poisson process (as in Nakano 2023). Use a temporal split — train ≤ 2014, test 2015–2018 — so the Brier score is directly comparable with Nakano's 0.100 (model) vs 0.136 (climatology) for hourly probabilities.
2. Report per horizon: Brier score and Brier skill score vs climatology, reliability diagram (Nakano over-forecast in the 0.4–0.8 bin is the failure mode to watch), ROC-AUC (Maimaiti 0.82–0.83 on balanced 60-min sets — report on the natural 1:6 imbalance as well), and precision/recall at the alert threshold. Evaluate separately for isolated onsets (≥ 3 h gap) — the ones that matter to a viewer — and for ΔSML ≥ 300 nT events.
3. Ablations: drop G (loading), drop the speed dependence, drop T, to confirm each term adds skill; the expectation from the literature is that v and loading carry most of the skill and T very little.
4. Local skill: compare IL* detections with the SML list to measure the local capture fraction and timing offset; expect ≈ 30 % capture and a few-minute lag.
5. Online: log every issued P_30/60/120 with the eventual outcome; recompute reliability monthly; freeze thresholds until ≥ 200 local onsets are logged.
